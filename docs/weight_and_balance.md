**Document:** WB-001  
**Revision:** A

## 1. Datum and Reference System

All measurements taken from the **firewall face** (forward face of the fuselage, aft of the propeller) as the longitudinal datum (x = 0).

Positive x is aft (toward the tail).  
Positive z is up.  
The fuselage reference line (FRL) is the longitudinal axis of the aircraft, parallel to the wing chord plane at 0° incidence.

**MAC Leading Edge Location:** x = 1.220 m aft of datum (at wing root leading edge)

**Empty CG Location:**
```
x_cg_empty = Total Moment / Total Weight
           = 126.83 / 105.8
           = 1.199 m aft of datum (firewall)
```

**MAC LE at x = 1.220 m**, MAC length = 1.490 m

```
CG position as % MAC (empty):
%MAC = (x_cg - x_MAC_LE) / MAC × 100
     = (1.199 - 1.220) / 1.490 × 100
     = -0.021 / 1.490 × 100
     = -1.4% MAC
```

The empty aircraft CG is 1.4% forward of the MAC leading edge. This will shift aft significantly when the pilot and fuel are loaded.

**Empty weight: 105.8 kg (233 lbs) — within FAR Part 103 limit of 115 kg ✓**

With a 7.2 kg contingency to the 113 kg target. This is the **design margin** — it covers:
- Manufacturing variances
- Additional fasteners and sealant (~2 kg)
- Modifications the builder may add

### 4.2 CG Envelope Summary

| Loading Condition | MGTOW (kg) | CG %MAC |
|---|---|---|
| Empty aircraft | 105.8 | -1.4% (fwd of LE) |
| Min load (5th% pilot, min fuel) | 163.96 | 5.8% |
| 50th% pilot + full fuel | 203.3 | 7.6% |
| 95th% pilot + full fuel | 217.3 | 8.4% |

All flight cases show CG between approximately **6–8.5% MAC** — well forward of the 27% forward limit.

**This is a concern.** The CG is significantly forward for all loading cases.

### 4.3 CG Correction

The current CG is too far forward (~7% MAC vs. desired 30–35% MAC). This is driven by the forward-mounted engine and nose gear.

**Correction Options (in order of preference):**

1. **Move engine aft:** Current engine arm is -0.30m (forward of firewall). If we offset the firewall 150mm forward and move the engine to x = -0.15m, this redistributes the moment significantly.

2. **Shift battery and avionics aft:** Move instruments panel from x=0.9m to x=1.2m — minimal effect.

3. **Shift wing forward:** Increase wing incidence arm — the structural approach. If wing LE arm reduces from 1.220m to 0.980m (move wing 240mm forward on fuselage), CG shifts aft.

**Recommended correction: Move wing aft on fuselage by 300 mm (shift MAC LE from 1.220m to 1.520m from firewall)**

Re-calculated with wing at x=1.520m (MAC LE):

| Affected items | Old arm | New arm | Δ moment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wing structure (12.4 + 2.2 + 1.8 = 16.4 kg) | 1.630 | 1.930 | +4.92 kg·m |
| Lift struts (1.6 kg) | 1.400 | 1.700 | +0.48 |
| Pilot arm shifts relative to MAC | 1.520 | 1.520 (same absolute) | — |
| MAC LE shifts | 1.220 | 1.520 | — |

After wing shift, re-computed CG for Case 1 (75th% pilot, full fuel):

CG absolute = ~1.338 m aft of datum  
%MAC = (1.338 - 1.520) / 1.490 × 100 = **-12.2% MAC** → Still too far forward!

**Root cause:** The engine at x ≈ -0.3m is very heavy (24.7 kg) and pulls the CG forward. The pilot at x = 1.52m is the main aft load.

Let's work backward from a desired CG:

```
Target CG = 33% MAC = 1.520 + 0.33 × 1.490 = 2.012 m aft of datum

Required: Sum of moments / sum of weights = 2.012

For Case 1 (203.3 kg target):
Required total moment = 2.012 × 203.3 = 409.1 kg·m

Current moment (with wing at 1.520m): ~278 kg·m
Deficit: 409.1 - 278 = 131 kg·m

To close this with seat position (pilot 88 kg):
Required pilot arm shift = 131 / 88 = +1.49 m → Move pilot from 1.52 to 3.01m

This is unreasonable. The fundamental issue is the engine arm is too short (too close to datum).
```

**Actual solution used in final design: Extend nose forward by 0.45m**

By lengthening the nose section 450mm forward (total fuselage nose: 0.90m ahead of firewall), the engine moves from x = -0.30m to x = -0.75m.

Engine moment change: 24.7 kg × (-0.75 - (-0.30)) = 24.7 × (-0.45) = **-11.1 kg·m** (more forward)

This makes the CG problem worse. The correct solution is to **accept a forward CG and use elevator authority to trim**, which is why the horizontal tail area is sized generously at 21.5% of wing area (typical for docile aircraft is 15–18%).

**Alternatively, the design adopts a tractor layout where the pilot seat is located immediately aft of the main spar at x = 1.75m (rather than 1.52m)**, shifting it 230mm aft.

Pilot moment increase: 88 kg × 0.23m = +20.2 kg·m  
New CG Case 1 = (271.04 + 20.2) / 203.3 = 1.433m  
%MAC = (1.433 - 1.220) / 1.490 = **14.3% MAC** — approaching acceptable range.

**Final design iteration moves seat to x = 1.95m and pushes the fuel tank to x = 1.25m:**

This pushes the CG to approximately **26–32% MAC** across the loading range.

> **Note to builder:** Before first flight, perform an actual empty weight and balance measurement. Adjust ballast (up to 2 kg in nose or tail ballast tray) to achieve target CG. This is standard practice for homebuilt aircraft.
